# Home Buying Guide

## Steps in the buying process

1. **Get pre-approved.** Before touring homes, get a mortgage pre-approval letter from a lender. It tells you your realistic budget and makes your offers credible. Pre-approval usually takes 1–3 days and requires income documents, bank statements, and a credit check.
2. **Define your search.** Area, budget, bedrooms, must-haves vs nice-to-haves. Expect to compromise on roughly one of: location, size, or condition.
3. **Tour homes.** Visit in person or virtually. In a fast market, plan to see new listings within the first few days.
4. **Make an offer.** Your agent prepares a purchase contract with price, earnest money, option period, financing terms, and closing date.
5. **Option period and inspection.** In Texas, buyers typically pay a small option fee for an unrestricted right to terminate during the option period (commonly 5–10 days). Schedule the general inspection immediately.
6. **Appraisal and underwriting.** The lender orders an appraisal and finalizes loan approval. Avoid new debt, job changes, or large unexplained deposits during this stage.
7. **Closing.** Final walkthrough, then sign at the title company. Bring a cashier's check or wire for your cash to close. You get keys when the deal funds.

## How making an offer works

An offer includes the price, the earnest money deposit (typically about 1% of price, credited to you at closing), the option fee and option period length, any seller concessions requested, the financing type, and the target closing date. The seller can accept, reject, or counter. Once both sides sign, the contract is "executed" and timelines start. Competitive situations may involve escalation clauses, larger option fees, shorter option periods, or appraisal gap coverage — discuss the tradeoffs with your agent before offering.

## Earnest money vs option fee

Earnest money (about 1% of the purchase price) shows good faith and is held by the title company; you generally get it back if you terminate within your contractual rights, and it's credited toward your costs at closing. The option fee (often $100–$500 in Texas) buys the unrestricted right to walk away during the option period; it is usually non-refundable but credited at closing.

## Closing costs for buyers

Buyer closing costs typically run 2–5% of the purchase price on top of the down payment. They include lender fees, title insurance, appraisal, survey (if new one needed), prepaid property taxes and insurance, and escrow setup. Sellers sometimes contribute to buyer closing costs as a negotiated concession.

## Down payments

- Conventional loans: as little as 3–5% down for qualified buyers; 20% down avoids private mortgage insurance (PMI).
- FHA loans: 3.5% down with more flexible credit requirements.
- VA loans (eligible veterans/service members): 0% down, no PMI.
- Putting less than 20% down is common — most first-time buyers do. PMI typically costs 0.3–1.5% of the loan per year and drops off once you reach 20% equity.

## First-time buyer tips

- Get pre-approved before falling in love with a house.
- Budget beyond the mortgage: property taxes (Texas has no state income tax but relatively high property taxes), insurance, HOA dues, maintenance (~1% of home value/year).
- Don't skip the inspection, even on new construction.
- Ask about property tax exemptions — the homestead exemption lowers the taxable value of your primary residence and caps annual assessed-value increases.
