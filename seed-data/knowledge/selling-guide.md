# Home Selling Guide

## Preparing your home for sale

Before listing: declutter and depersonalize, handle small repairs (leaky faucets, scuffed paint, broken fixtures), deep clean, and boost curb appeal. Homes that are professionally photographed and well-staged consistently sell faster and closer to asking price. Your agent will advise whether pre-listing improvements (paint, flooring, landscaping) are worth the cost in your neighborhood — many big renovations don't return their cost at sale.

## Pricing strategy

Pricing is set by comparable recent sales ("comps") in your area — similar size, condition, and location within the last 3–6 months. Overpricing is the most common seller mistake: overpriced homes sit, accumulate days-on-market stigma, and often sell below what correct initial pricing would have achieved. Your agent prepares a comparative market analysis (CMA) to recommend a list price and strategy (price at market, slightly under to drive multiple offers, or slightly above in a low-inventory pocket).

## Costs of selling

Sellers typically pay: agent commissions (negotiable, commonly 5–6% total split between both sides' agents), title policy (customarily seller-paid in Texas), prorated property taxes, any negotiated buyer concessions or repairs, mortgage payoff, and moving costs. Net proceeds = sale price minus these costs minus your remaining loan balance. Ask for a seller net sheet to see the estimate for your specific situation.

## The selling timeline

1. Listing consultation and CMA (week 0)
2. Prep, photos, and go-live (1–3 weeks)
3. Showings and offers (days to weeks depending on market)
4. Contract execution, buyer option period and inspection (about 1 week)
5. Repair negotiations, appraisal, buyer financing (2–4 weeks)
6. Closing and funding (typically 30–45 days after contract)

## Handling offers and negotiations

Evaluate offers on more than price: financing strength (cash vs conventional vs FHA), down payment size, option period length, leaseback needs, contingencies (especially a home-sale contingency), and proposed closing date. A slightly lower cash offer with a short option period can be stronger than a higher financed offer with weak pre-approval. You can accept, counter one buyer, or call for "highest and best" from multiple buyers.

## Disclosures

Texas sellers must complete a Seller's Disclosure Notice describing the property's condition and known issues (foundation repairs, flooding, roof age, insurance claims, etc.). Disclose honestly — failure to disclose known material defects is the top source of post-sale legal disputes.
