# Frequently Asked Questions

## How do property viewings work?

You can request a viewing through this assistant — we need your name, a contact method, and a preferred time. An agent confirms the appointment, usually within a few business hours. Tours typically take 20–40 minutes. Virtual tours are available for most listings if you're relocating from out of town.

## Do I need an agent to buy a home?

You're not legally required to have one, but a buyer's agent handles search, showings, offer strategy, negotiations, and deadline tracking. Ask about agent compensation up front — how buyer-agent fees are paid is negotiated and disclosed in your representation agreement.

## What credit score do I need to buy?

Conventional loans generally want 620+, FHA can go lower (580+ with 3.5% down), and the best rates typically go to 740+. A lender can pre-qualify you quickly — a slightly lower score usually means a rate adjustment, not a denial.

## How much house can I afford?

A common rule of thumb: total housing cost (mortgage, taxes, insurance, HOA) around 28% of gross monthly income, with total debts under 36–43%. In Texas, remember property taxes are a large share of the monthly payment — often 1.6–2.2% of the home's value per year depending on the county and exemptions. Get pre-approved for a precise number.

## What are property taxes like in the Austin area?

Combined rates vary by city, county, and school district — typically in the 1.6–2.2% range of assessed value annually. The homestead exemption reduces taxable value on a primary residence and caps assessed increases at 10% per year. New buyers should protest their assessment if it exceeds the purchase price.

## What is an HOA and should I worry about it?

A homeowners association maintains shared amenities and enforces neighborhood rules, funded by dues (from ~$30/month in suburban subdivisions to $500+ in downtown condos). Before buying, review the HOA's budget, reserves, rules, and any pending special assessments — we can request these documents during your option period.

## Is now a good time to buy or sell?

Market timing depends on rates, inventory, and your personal situation — nobody can promise where prices go. The better question is whether the monthly payment fits your budget and how long you plan to hold; owning tends to reward time-in-market rather than market-timing.
